# Before/After Examples

Each rewrite keeps every fact and real hedge from the source, and adds none.

## Example 1: Throat-Clearing + Binary Contrast

**Before:**

> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**

> "Building products is hard because people are complex."

**Changes:** Removed the opener, the "not because X, because Y" reversal, and the emphasis crutch. The claim stays: the difficulty comes from people.

---

## Example 2: Filler + Unnecessary Reassurance

**Before:**

> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**

> "Most teams struggle with alignment, and few people want to admit they're confused."

**Changes:** Cut both throat-clearing phrases and the permission-granting ending. "Most" stays: it is the writer's scope, and "Teams struggle" would claim more. "Nobody" became "few people" because the writer did not mean it literally.

---

## Example 3: Business Jargon Stack

**Before:**

> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**

> "Decide before the picture is clear. Your competitors won't wait for you."

**Changes:** Under the jargon sits one instruction (act under uncertainty) and one reason (competitors move). The rewrite says those two things and nothing new. If the piece is about a specific decision, name it instead.

---

## Example 4: Dramatic Fragmentation

**Before:**

> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**

> "Of speed, quality, and low cost, you get two."

**Changes:** One sentence instead of six fragments. The list keeps three items because the content has three.

---

## Example 5: Rhetorical Setup

**Before:**

> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**

> "The best teams optimize for learning over productivity."

**Changes:** Dropped the question, the preview, and the prompt to think. The comparison with productivity is the claim, so it stays in one plain clause.

---

## Example 6: False Agency With a Missing Actor

The paragraph before this one says that Priya's team owns the export feature.

**Before:**

> "Within a week of launch, the complaint about CSV encoding became a fix. Shortly after, the decision emerged to retire the old export flow."

**After:**

> "Priya's team fixed the CSV encoding complaint within a week of launch. Shortly after, [who?] decided to retire the old export flow."

**Note to the writer:** The text does not say who decided to retire the old flow. Tell me and I'll fill it in.

**Changes:** The text names the fixer, so the first sentence uses it. It names no decider, so the rewrite asks instead of crediting Priya's team.

---

## Example 7: Light Pass on a Human Draft

A person wrote this newsletter paragraph and asked for a light pass.

**Before:**

> "Here's the thing: I've rewritten our onboarding doc four times this year — and every version was worse than the last, which is honestly a talent. This time I just deleted half of it. Nobody has complained yet, which either means it worked or nobody reads it."

**After:**

> "I've rewritten our onboarding doc four times this year, and every version was worse than the last, which is a talent. This time I deleted half of it. Nobody has complained yet, which either means it worked or nobody reads it."

**Changes:** Removed the opener, the em dash, "honestly", and "just". The joke, the first person, and the closing line stay. "Nobody" stays too: the writer means it literally, and it carries the punch line.
